Reviews in brackets count only judges whose marks carry signal: two or more scores that aren't all the same. A project flagged as resting on fewer than two of those is ranked mostly on its other reviewers; a third review would settle it.

How the score is made

  1. Weighted mark. Each judge's marks are combined with the rubric weights, on the {{ config.scale_min }} to {{ config.scale_max }} scale.
  2. Per-judge z-score. Each mark is compared with that judge's own average and spread, so a harsh judge's 3 and a generous judge's 4.5 can mean the same thing. The spread is shrunk toward everyone's (kappa {{ results.kappa|floatformat:"-1" }}), so a judge with two scores can't swing a result.
  3. Average. A project's z is the mean over its judges, shown back on the familiar scale (overall mean {{ results.pop_mean|floatformat:2 }}, spread {{ results.pop_std|floatformat:2 }}).

The full argument, and the test showing it ranks closer to the truth than a raw average, is in JUDGING.md.
